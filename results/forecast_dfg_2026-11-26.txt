RUTY atom: forecast for the job-graph (DFG) scheduler, filed BEFORE the measurement (see the file time stamp; do not edit after the run).

Job graph on the 4-atom image (400 MHz): 22 XMSS trees of SLH-DSA-128f in SHAKE256 (6 dependent stages per tree, the chain stage runs inside
the atom), plus B tag nodes (2400 MiniSHAKE128 tags each, split atoms) and F firmware nodes (1 MB TurboSHAKE128 each, one wide atom per image).
Three policies on the same graph and the same data:
  serial      : one tenant after another, each on all atoms (trees, then tags, then firmware)
  graph       : any ready node on any free atom, tenants alternate, no limit on stream nodes
  graph+rule  : stream nodes may load the port up to its measured limit and no further (tags = 1 unit, firmware = 2 units, budget 2);
                every other atom goes to tree nodes
Known from earlier runs: trees alone on 4 atoms 2.48 ms; tags about 23.5 M/s at the port limit; firmware about 2.2 to 2.4 GB/s at the port limit.

Forecast, mix B=8, F=2 (streams need about 1.7 ms of port time):
  serial      4.0 to 4.6 ms
  graph+rule  3.1 to 3.8 ms, that is 12% to 28% below serial
  graph       within 6% of graph+rule (either side)
Forecast, mix B=4, F=1: graph+rule 8% to 20% below serial.   Forecast, mix B=16, F=4: graph+rule 12% to 30% below serial.
Forecast, trees only (B=0, F=0): all three policies within 3% of each other and within 5% of 2.48 ms.
Atom utilization (share of atom time spent running a node): higher under graph+rule than under serial in every mix with streams.
All roots, tags and firmware digests correct under every policy.
The claim "a scheduler that knows the limits does better than first-come" FAILS if graph+rule is not at least 8% faster than serial at B=8, F=2,
or if any digest is wrong.
